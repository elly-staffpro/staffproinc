Monthly Bulletin

HR & Compliance News

A monthly roundup of what's changing in HR, employment law, payroll, and compliance — curated for business owners.

August 2026

This month brings significant developments in workplace safety regulation, employment trends, and retirement benefits that demand attention from employers. From federal heat protection debates to slower hiring patterns and important changes to retirement planning, business leaders need to stay informed about shifting compliance landscapes. Here's what you need to know to keep your organization on track.

Workplace Safety

Federal Heat Protection Standards Face Congressional Pushback Amid Record Temperatures

A Republican-controlled House committee has advanced legislation that would prohibit the U.S. Labor Department from implementing federal heat protection standards for workers. This development comes as the country experiences dangerous heat waves, creating uncertainty for employers about their obligations to protect outdoor and indoor workers from heat-related illness.

Takeaway: Until federal standards are resolved, employers should proactively implement heat safety protocols including water breaks, shade access, and acclimatization periods to protect workers and minimize liability.
HR Compliance

Federal Court Rules OSHA Cannot Require Mental Illness Reporting on Workplace Injury Logs

A federal appeals court in Texas has determined that OSHA lacks authority to mandate employers document work-related mental illnesses on their injury and illness logs. The decision represents a significant limitation on OSHA's workplace safety recordkeeping requirements and creates confusion about mental health injury reporting obligations.

Takeaway: Review your injury recordkeeping practices with legal counsel to ensure compliance with this new ruling while still maintaining documentation that protects your organization in workers' compensation and liability matters.
Employment Law

Weekly Job Growth Slows to 15,000 According to Latest ADP Employment Data

The ADP National Employment Report shows U.S. private employers added an average of only 15,000 jobs per week in the four weeks ending July 11, 2026, indicating a significant slowdown in hiring activity. This deceleration in employment growth suggests businesses are taking a more cautious approach to workforce expansion.

Takeaway: Use this slower hiring environment to focus on retention of top performers and ensure your compensation packages remain competitive enough to keep the talent you already have.
Employee Benefits

Retirement Planning Gets More Complex as EPF Reform and Social Security Concerns Converge

Recent reforms to the Employees' Provident Fund combined with growing concerns about Social Security's long-term solvency are pushing employers to reconsider their retirement benefit strategies. These changes create both compliance obligations and opportunities to redesign retirement offerings that better balance security with growth potential.

Takeaway: Schedule a benefits review to evaluate whether your retirement offerings align with current regulations and meet employee needs, particularly for younger workers concerned about Social Security's future.
Workers' Comp

Workers' Compensation System Failures Leave Injured Employees Without Adequate Support

A comprehensive analysis reveals that America's workers' compensation system is increasingly failing to provide adequate support for employees injured on the job, with benefit levels eroding and claims processes becoming more difficult to navigate. These systemic problems create both ethical concerns and potential legal exposure for employers who rely on workers' comp as their sole protection strategy.

Takeaway: Beyond maintaining required workers' comp coverage, consider supplemental accident insurance and return-to-work programs that demonstrate genuine commitment to injured employees and reduce litigation risk.
