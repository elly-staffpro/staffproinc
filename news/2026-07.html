Monthly Bulletin

HR & Compliance News

A monthly roundup of what's changing in HR, employment law, payroll, and compliance — curated for business owners.

July 2026

This month brings significant developments affecting how employers manage their workforce, from updates to India's labor code implementation to concerns about Social Security's long-term viability. We're also seeing important conversations around workplace safety communication, apprenticeship expansion, and innovative technology solutions for global employment operations. Here's what small and mid-size businesses need to know.

HR Compliance

India Rolls Out New Labour Codes, Creating Compliance Challenges for Companies with Operations There

India officially notified its consolidated Labour Code in May 2026, replacing dozens of older labor laws with four unified codes covering wages, industrial relations, social security, and workplace safety. Companies operating in India or employing workers there must now navigate new payroll structures, statutory deductions, and compliance reporting requirements. Service providers like Hemiton Global are stepping in to help businesses manage the transition.

Takeaway: If your company has employees, contractors, or operations in India, review your current compliance practices immediately to ensure alignment with the new Labour Code framework.

The new Labour Code represents India's most significant labor law reform in decades, consolidating approximately 29 separate labor statutes into four comprehensive codes. The changes affect fundamental employment practices including how wages are calculated, what constitutes taxable income versus allowances, and how employee benefits must be structured. For U.S. companies with development teams, customer support operations, or back-office functions in India, this creates both complexity and risk. The new rules change statutory contribution rates for programs like the Employees' Provident Fund and modify thresholds that determine which businesses must comply with certain regulations. Companies that fail to update their payroll systems and employment contracts could face penalties and employee disputes. Many businesses are turning to Professional Employer Organizations and specialized compliance providers to manage the technical requirements, particularly around monthly and annual statutory filings that now follow new formats and schedules.

Employee Benefits

Social Security Trust Fund Projected to Deplete by 2032, Raising Questions About Future Benefit Levels

The Social Security Administration's latest trustees report projects the program's trust fund will be exhausted by 2032, eight years from now. Once depleted, incoming payroll taxes would only cover approximately 77% of scheduled benefits, triggering automatic cuts unless Congress acts. This timeline has significant implications for retirement planning and employee financial wellness programs.

Takeaway: Consider enhancing your retirement benefits communication and financial wellness resources to help employees understand they may need to rely less heavily on Social Security than previous generations.

Social Security operates on a pay-as-you-go system where current workers' payroll taxes fund current retirees' benefits, unlike corporate pensions that build dedicated reserve funds. As baby boomers continue retiring in large numbers while birth rates remain lower, fewer workers are supporting more beneficiaries. The 2032 depletion date means the trust fund reserves built up during higher-earning years will run out, leaving only ongoing tax collections to fund benefits. While Social Security won't disappear entirely, the projected 23% benefit reduction would significantly impact retirees who depend on it for most of their income. For employers, this creates urgency around retirement readiness. Younger employees especially need to understand that Social Security likely won't provide the same income replacement their parents received. Smart employers are responding by improving 401(k) matching, offering financial planning resources, and conducting retirement readiness workshops. These proactive steps help employees prepare for a future where Social Security provides a smaller safety net, while also strengthening your benefits package as a recruitment and retention tool.

Workplace Safety

Manufacturing Survey Reveals Communication Breakdowns Contributing to Safety Risks and Turnover

A new industry survey found that communication failures on factory floors are contributing to safety incidents, production disruptions, high turnover, and slower technology adoption. Many frontline manufacturing workers aren't receiving critical updates about policy changes, safety protocols, or operational adjustments, creating risks for both employees and employers. The research highlights the challenge of reaching workers who don't sit at desks or regularly check email.

Takeaway: Evaluate how effectively your company communicates safety updates and policy changes to frontline employees, and consider implementing tools designed for deskless workers such as mobile apps, digital signage, or team huddles.

The survey underscores a persistent problem in manufacturing and other industries with frontline workforces: traditional communication methods don't work for employees who spend their shifts on production lines, in warehouses, or in the field. When safety protocol updates, equipment changes, or policy modifications don't reach workers effectively, the consequences range from minor inefficiencies to serious injuries. The research also found that poor communication slows the adoption of new technologies, including AI-powered tools that could improve productivity and safety. Workers who don't understand why changes are happening or how new systems benefit them are naturally more resistant. For small and mid-size manufacturers, the solution doesn't necessarily require expensive enterprise software. Effective approaches include brief daily or shift-start huddles where supervisors share key updates, strategically placed digital displays showing safety metrics and announcements, mobile-friendly communication apps that workers can access during breaks, and visual management boards that display critical information right on the production floor. The key is meeting workers where they are rather than expecting them to seek out information through channels designed for office employees.

Employment Law

Apprenticeship Programs Gaining Momentum but Experts Say More Support Needed for Widespread Adoption

Registered apprenticeship programs are expanding across the United States as employers seek solutions to skills gaps and workforce development challenges. However, experts warn that without additional funding, updated regulations, and better data collection, the U.S. won't achieve the robust apprenticeship system seen in countries like Germany and Switzerland. Current barriers include complex registration processes and limited awareness among small employers.

Takeaway: If your company struggles to find qualified workers in skilled trades or technical roles, explore registered apprenticeship programs as a way to build your own talent pipeline while potentially accessing tax credits and grants.

Apprenticeships combine on-the-job training with classroom instruction, allowing workers to earn while they learn and employers to develop employees with exactly the skills their business needs. The model works particularly well in manufacturing, healthcare, information technology, and skilled trades, but remains underutilized in the United States compared to other developed nations. Recent growth is encouraging, driven partly by demographic shifts that are reducing the available labor pool and partly by recognition that traditional four-year degrees don't suit every career path or every learner. For small and mid-size businesses, apprenticeships offer a solution to the perpetual problem of entry-level candidates who lack practical experience. Instead of competing for the limited pool of experienced workers, you develop your own. The process does require investment in training infrastructure and mentor time, but registered apprenticeships often qualify for state and federal tax credits, grant funding, and technical assistance. The registration process, while sometimes bureaucratic, provides structure and credibility that helps with recruitment. As advocacy grows for streamlined registration procedures and increased funding, now is a strategic time for employers to explore whether apprenticeships could address persistent hiring challenges in their organizations.

HR Compliance

New AI-Powered Platform Automates Global Employment Tasks for Companies Using International Contractors

Remote People launched Command Center, described as the first action-taking AI assistant for Employer of Record operations. Unlike advisory AI tools, this system can execute employment actions such as salary changes, terminations, and multi-country onboarding in plain language across more than 180 countries. The technology represents a significant shift from AI that provides information to AI that completes complex compliance tasks.

Takeaway: If your business employs international workers through an EOR, stay informed about emerging AI tools that could streamline administration while ensuring any automated system still maintains proper oversight and compliance standards.

Employer of Record services allow companies to hire employees in countries where they don't have a legal entity, with the EOR handling payroll, benefits, tax compliance, and employment law adherence. Traditionally, making changes to international employees' status, compensation, or employment requires submitting requests through tickets or forms, waiting for human processors to handle compliance checks, and managing time zone delays. Command Center aims to automate much of this workflow using AI that understands natural language requests, knows the employment laws and tax rules for each country, and can execute compliant actions automatically. For example, an HR manager could theoretically type "increase Maria's salary to €65,000 effective next month" and the system would handle the pay change, tax adjustments, contract amendments, and statutory notifications required in that specific country. This kind of automation could dramatically reduce administrative burden for companies managing globally distributed teams. However, it also raises important questions about oversight, error correction, and whether fully automated employment decisions might create new legal risks. The technology is very new, and employers should approach it thoughtfully, ensuring that speed and convenience don't come at the expense of accuracy, compliance, or the human judgment that complex employment situations often require.
