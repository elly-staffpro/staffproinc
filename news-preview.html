HR & Compliance News

A monthly roundup of what's changing in HR, employment law, payroll, and compliance — curated for business owners.

May 2026

May brought significant activity on the employment law front, with new federal guidance on overtime thresholds, updated ACA reporting requirements, and a notable push in several states toward expanded paid leave mandates. Here's what business owners need to know heading into the summer.

Employment Law

DOL Proposes Raising Overtime Salary Threshold to $58,000

The Department of Labor has proposed increasing the minimum salary for overtime-exempt employees from $43,888 to $58,656 annually. If finalized, employers would need to either raise affected employees' salaries or reclassify them as hourly and begin tracking hours worked.

Takeaway: Review your salaried employees now — anyone earning between $44K and $59K may need reclassification if this rule is finalized.
Payroll & Tax

IRS Updates W-4 Withholding Tables for Second Half of 2026

The IRS released updated federal income tax withholding tables effective July 1, 2026, reflecting adjustments for inflation. Employers using payroll software should confirm their provider has applied the update, as incorrect withholding can create issues for employees at tax time.

Takeaway: Confirm with your payroll provider that the new withholding tables will be applied to your July payroll runs.
Employee Benefits

ACA Reporting Deadlines Remain Unchanged for 2026 Tax Year

The IRS confirmed that Affordable Care Act reporting deadlines for the 2026 tax year will remain the same — Forms 1095-C must be furnished to employees by January 31, 2027, with electronic filing due by April 1, 2027. Applicable large employers (50+ full-time equivalents) must comply or face per-form penalties.

Takeaway: If you have 50 or more full-time equivalent employees, make sure your ACA tracking is current — don't wait until year-end.
Workplace Safety

OSHA Increases Focus on Heat Illness Prevention Ahead of Summer

OSHA announced an expanded enforcement initiative targeting heat-related illness in outdoor and indoor workplaces this summer. Inspectors will be conducting targeted inspections at worksites where employees are exposed to high heat, including warehouses, construction sites, and food processing facilities.

Takeaway: Review your heat illness prevention plan now — ensure employees have access to water, rest breaks, and shade, and that supervisors are trained to recognize symptoms.
HR Compliance

New York Expands Paid Prenatal Leave to All Private Employers

New York State's paid prenatal personal leave law, which took effect in January 2025, continues to see compliance enforcement ramp up. All private-sector employers in New York — regardless of size — must provide up to 20 hours of paid leave per year for prenatal medical appointments. It runs separately from PFL and sick leave.

Takeaway: Make sure your leave policy documents this benefit separately from PFL and sick leave, and that managers are aware it cannot be denied.
