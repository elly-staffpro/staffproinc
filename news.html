Monthly Bulletin

HR & Compliance News

A monthly roundup of what's changing in HR, employment law, payroll, and compliance — curated for business owners.

September 2026

September brought a wave of federal rulemaking — some of it highly technical, most of it aimed at agencies or multinational corporations rather than the employers we work with. A few items stand out: new car loan interest deductions that touch payroll questions, clarified unemployment data rules, and ongoing guidance on workplace safety and tax-exempt organizations.

Payroll & Tax

New car loan interest deduction takes effect in November

The Treasury Department finalized a rule allowing certain taxpayers to deduct up to $10,000 in qualified passenger vehicle loan interest. The rule takes effect November 9 and applies to loans taken out after December 31, 2024. It also creates new information reporting requirements for lenders who receive $600 or more in interest payments on these loans.

Takeaway: Employers don't administer this deduction, but employees may ask payroll questions about it — worth knowing it exists.

Source: Federal Register (Treasury Department)

HR Compliance

Labor Department finalizes unemployment data-sharing rule

The Labor Department finalized a rule requiring states to share certain confidential unemployment compensation information with federal officials for program oversight and fraud detection. It takes effect November 16. The change sits mostly with state agencies rather than individual employers — but it signals closer scrutiny of unemployment claims data.

Takeaway: Nothing to do here, but worth knowing if you handle a lot of unemployment claims.

Source: Federal Register (Labor Department)

HR Compliance

Treasury proposes updated racial nondiscrimination standard for private schools

The Treasury Department proposed updating regulations to clarify that private schools lose tax-exempt status if they discriminate on the basis of race, color, or national or ethnic origin in their educational, admissions, scholarship, athletic, or other policies. A public hearing is scheduled for December 2, and the rule would take effect for taxable years beginning after May 31, 2027.

Takeaway: Relevant only to private schools seeking or holding tax-exempt status — not a general employer issue.

Source: Federal Register (Treasury Department)

Workplace Safety

Labor Department rescinds outdated farmworker enforcement coordination rules

The Labor Department rescinded regulations that established formal coordination procedures among its Wage and Hour Division, OSHA, and Employment and Training Administration for migrant and seasonal farmworker enforcement. The rule takes effect September 30. The Department says the regulations no longer reflect its structure or practices and that it will rely on flexible coordination models already in use.

Takeaway: Affects agricultural employers who work with migrant or seasonal labor — the agencies will still coordinate, just without the formal rulebook.

Source: Federal Register (Labor Department)

Workplace Safety

Chicago construction employer cited for repeat fall protection failures

OSHA cited a Calumet City construction employer with willful, repeat, and serious violations after two separate investigations found workers installing roof rafters and sheeting without fall protection, exposing them to falls of up to 25 feet. The agency proposed $265,868 in combined penalties. The employer has 15 days to comply, request a conference, or contest the findings.

Takeaway: A reminder that repeat violations — especially willful ones — carry heavy penalties and continued scrutiny.

Source: U.S. Department of Labor
